Bass — controls the low rumble (kick drums, bass guitar, chest voice). Boost for more punch, cut to clean up muddiness.
Treble — controls the high sparkle (hi-hats, breath, brightness). Boost for crispness, cut for warmth.
Reverb — adds room/space simulation. Wet/Dry controls how much reverb vs. original you hear. Room size controls how big the space sounds — small = bathroom tile, large = concert hall.
Echo — a repeat of the sound. Delay time = how long before the echo comes back. Feedback = how many times it repeats (high feedback = many echoes fading out).
Super Clear — a two-part enhancer. Presence boosts the 3–4kHz range where vocals and instruments "cut through." Air boosts 12kHz+ for a shimmery open top-end.

The EQ lets you boost or cut specific frequency ranges. Each band is a different part of the sound spectrum — think of it like separate volume knobs for different parts of the music.
60–120 Hz
Sub-bass & bass. Kick drum thud, bass guitar rumble. Boost for more power, cut if muddy.
250–500 Hz
Low-mids. Body of vocals, warmth. Boost for fullness, cut to reduce boxiness.
1k–2.5k Hz
Upper-mids. Presence, attack, clarity. Where vocals and guitars "cut through."
8k–16k Hz
Highs & air. Brightness, sibilance, shimmer. Boost for crispness, cut if too harsh.
